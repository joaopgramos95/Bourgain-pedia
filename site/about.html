Method

How a paper gets from the bibliography into the toolkit, and what the site does and does not claim.

The pipeline

Everything here runs one paper at a time, through the skill $bourgainize (Codex) / /bourgainize (Claude Code):

  1. Choose. A paper is picked from the bibliography and confirmed exactly — he has near-identical titles a decade apart.
  2. Download. arXiv source if it exists, then arXiv PDF, then an open-access copy, then NUMDAM / EuDML / Euclid, then the DOI. Files land in <year>/<slug>/.
  3. Expand, under Blueprint A, into <name>-expanded.tex.
  4. Stop and ask. The expansion stands on its own; the harvest is separate work and needs an explicit yes.
  5. Harvest, under Blueprint B, into ideas-<name>.md, and reconcile against the toolkit.

Blueprint A — the article

The digestion is his paper at full length, not a survey of it and not a better proof. Six rules govern it:

  1. When in doubt, expand. Doubt is the trigger, not a reason to hesitate.
  2. Do not deviate from the line of work in the article. Stick to his arguments.
  3. Introducing notation in order to explain the article is fine — declared, and attributed to us.
  4. The expanded article is strictly larger than the original.
  5. No computation in the original is omitted from the new one.
  6. Arguments left out of the original are explained in the new one.

Rule 6 is the one that does the work. One checks easily, a standard argument gives, by the same reasoning, it is well known that — each is a hole, and each is logged in a ledger and filled with an actual proof. The digestion is finished when every ledger row is closed.

Where a step cannot be closed, it is marked caution in the PDF and OPEN-GAP in the ledger, and it stays visible. A plausible reconstruction is never promoted into a proof.

Blueprint B — the ideas

Blueprint B reads the expansion, never the original: the point of the expansion is that the moves he performed silently are now on the page.

  • Pass 1, total harvest. Line by line. Every step where something is done gets a keyword and a note on what it buys. Over-collect; 80–200 rows is normal.
  • Pass 2, discard the routine. Triangle inequality, Cauchy–Schwarz, Hölder, Fubini, geometric series — out. Unless something about how the move is used was a choice, in which case the row says what the non-obvious part was.
  • Pass 3, prune again. Cut rows that describe subject matter rather than technique, and rows that fail the question could I apply this in another paper? Repeat until a sweep changes nothing — usually three sweeps in all.
  • Pass 4, reconcile. Existing idea, extended gloss, or new entry; the paper's id joins the idea's list.

A score is never written by hand. It is the length of an idea's paper list, recomputed on every build.

The bibliography

Merged from three open sources. zbMATH Open is the spine: a disambiguated mathematical bibliography that is complete back to 1976 and carries the early French and Belgian seminar papers the citation indices miss. OpenAlex supplies citation counts, DOIs and open-access copies. arXiv supplies preprints. Records are matched on DOI first, then on a normalised title within a ±3-year window; a handful of OCR-mangled titles and chapter-level duplicates are reconciled by a curated map in tools/build_data.py, which lists each one and why.

Citation counts are OpenAlex counts, refreshed by hand about once a week with python3 tools/refresh_citations.py. The date they were last refreshed is in the footer of every page. They are a rough measure and are not comparable across decades: OpenAlex indexes a 2014 paper's citations far better than a 1980 one's, and a blank means "not indexed", not "never cited".

What is ours and what is not

  • Ours: every summary, every digestion, every toolkit entry and gloss, the collaborator prose.
  • Not ours: the bibliographic records, the citation counts, and the papers themselves. Links point to the publisher, arXiv, or a repository; no copyrighted full text is redistributed here.
  • Not written yet: shown as an explicit blank. A field is left empty rather than filled with a guess — including collaborator birth years and affiliations, which come from Wikidata and are simply absent where Wikidata has no confident match.

Running it

python3 tools/fetch_sources.py        # zbMATH + OpenAlex + arXiv -> data/raw/
python3 tools/build_data.py           # merge -> data/, site/data/, Papers/<year>.md
python3 tools/enrich_collaborators.py # Wikidata dates and affiliations
python3 tools/refresh_citations.py    # the weekly update
sh tools/serve.sh                     # http://localhost:8017/

The site is static and dependency-free; site/index.html also opens straight off the filesystem, since the data ships as JS globals rather than fetched JSON.
