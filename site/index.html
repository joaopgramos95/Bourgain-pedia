A collaborative reading project

Every paper Jean Bourgain wrote, read down to its ideas.

Bourgain left behind a body of work that is famously hard to enter: short papers, compressed arguments, whole techniques introduced in a paragraph. This project goes through them one at a time — expanding each paper into a version where nothing is omitted, then extracting the ideas it runs on and committing them back to a shared toolkit.

How to read this site

All articles is the spine: the complete bibliography, by year. Each entry carries its full reference, its citation count, a download link, and two slots this project fills in — a summary (our précis, not the abstract) and a digestion (the expanded paper we produce).

Bourgain's toolkit is where the work accumulates. Every idea and technique found in a digested paper — from orthogonality and stopping time up through sparseness and large deviations — gets a name, a short explanation, and a score: the number of papers it appears in.

Collaborators lists everyone who wrote a paper with him, A to Z, with a collaborator score.

Method describes the two blueprints — one for expanding an article, one for harvesting its ideas — and the /bourgainize skill that runs them.

Where the bibliography comes from

The list is merged from three open sources: zbMATH Open (the authoritative, disambiguated mathematical bibliography — the spine), OpenAlex (citation counts and open-access copies) and arXiv (preprints). Nothing on the article pages is written by a publisher: the summaries and digestions are ours.
